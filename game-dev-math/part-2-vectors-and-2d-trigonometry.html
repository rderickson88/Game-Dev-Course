Part 2 -Vectors and 2D Trigonometry

Vectors

So far we have talked mostly about points, which are great for static objects that exist on a screen and aren't going to be doing much.

Video games have interactive elements though, and we want to encourage both our systems and our players to be able to move stuff around and interact with things that are moving around!

That is where Vectors come in.

As discussed, we can interpret the position of any object in 2D space using X and Y coordinates

( X, Y )

A vector also receives similar X and Y components, but **magnitude**.

Magnitude refers to the direction of the vector, giving it an implied length.

Let's take a look at a concrete example with this graph

What we have here are two distinct points: Point A and Point B

We need to figure out two primary components to this vector. The first we will call it's displacement.

This is a simple as using the formula from Part 1 - Intro to Math Concepts for Game Design

(X2 - X1) (Y2 - Y1)

We take the two points and subtract the target point's components from the original point's components. Putting them together you'll get a new X and Y coordinate. For the sake of our argument here, let's assume that the result for the vector in our graph is:

(3, 4)

This represents the relative displacement along the X and Y axes for our two points. This doesn't completely describe the vector though, because we also need to account for its **magnitude**

Drawing in those lines, what we have is in fact, a RIGHT TRIANGLE.

That's where basic Trigonometry comes into play.

Why is this important?

In games, you will want to measure distances between things all the time. You will want to measure the distance between your player and a coin they might collect, or figure out how far a projectile might need to travel. For each one of these calculations, you will need to take the two points - one being origin and the other being destination - and you will have to figure out what that distance is going to have to be. Triangle are great for helping you to figure that out, with a little help from Pythagoras we can solve it.
